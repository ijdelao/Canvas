Welcome to the Course

This is an academic history course that surveys United States history from the Chicano perspective. We'll cover historical periods from the initial contact between European and indigenous North American societies through colonial, early national, and contemporary American periods—with emphasis on Chicano contributions to the development of the United States.

This course involves considerable data from comparative viewpoints. Keeping up with lectures and textbook readings is essential. I strongly recommend reading materials before each class lecture, since lectures will not repeat most of the information found in assigned readings. Come prepared to take notes, ask questions, and engage with the analogies and frameworks offered in class.

If you're having difficulty with course readings, reach out—I'm here to help. And yes, you can enjoy learning about Chicano history. I'll do my best to make sure you do!

Course Schedule

Overview of course content, introduction and framework of analysis. Cultural and historical background of the Chicano. Pre-colonial period: Native people of the Americas. Spaniards and the indigenous Americans.

Readings & Assignments

Class introductions

📖 Silencing the Past by Michel-Rolph Trouillot — Ch. 5: "Good Day, Columbus"

The Spanish defeat of the Aztec Empire. The Mission system and early expeditions to the Southwest.

Readings & Assignments

📖 On Savage Shores by Caroline Dodds — Ch. 1: "Slavery"

📜 Primary Sources: Letters of Cortés, Florentine Codex, Lienzo de Tlaxcala

The Mexican Far North. U.S. interest in Spanish and Mexican America. Early filibustering. Texas War of 1825–1836: A war of slavery—its impact on Chicano history.

Readings & Assignments

📖 Fierce and Unnatural Cruelty by Inga Clendinnen

Texas Republic. U.S. expansionist sentiments toward California and New Mexico. The Mexican American War, 1846–1848. Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo.

Readings & Assignments

📖 Earth, Wind and America by Michael Harriot

Political, social, economic, and cultural subjugation of La Raza in California. New Mexico, Texas and Arizona. Mexican American contributions to the development of the region.

Readings & Assignments

📖 The Lynching of Persons of Mexican Origin or Descent by William Carrigan

📜 Primary Sources: ⚠️ Graphic photographs and first-hand accounts of lynchings of Americans of Mexican descent

📖 The Alamo as Rupture

The Mexican Immigrant era, 1900–1929. The Mexican returns North—adaptation and survival in the United States. Labor rights movements and contributions of the immigrant era.

Readings & Assignments

📜 Primary Source: Human Betterment Foundation Letter to W.E.B. Du Bois

📖 Sterilization in the Name of Health by Alexandra Minna Stern

The Depression. Immigrant life in the United States. Gender, family, and cultural issues. Decade of Betrayal—Repatriation.
